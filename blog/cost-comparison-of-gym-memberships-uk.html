Cost Comparison of Gym Memberships UK

19 November 2026

A cost comparison of gym memberships in the UK comes down to three things: the monthly fee, the joining fee and how long you are tied in. Budget chains such as The Gym Group and PureGym start at under £20 a month, while the full-service health clubs we checked cost roughly £50 to £90, and prices change from one branch to the next.

Quick answer: When we checked the chains’ own websites in November 2026, budget gyms started from £15.99 a month (The Gym Group, off-peak) and £17.99 (PureGym, off-peak), while health clubs such as Bannatyne and Nuffield Health cost roughly £50 to £90 a month at the branches we looked at. Joining fees, contract length and cancellation terms change the real cost, so check them before you sign.

Key Takeaways

  • Budget chains start from £15.99–£17.99 a month off-peak; the full-service health clubs we checked cost roughly £50–£90 a month.

  • Major UK cities, especially London, often have higher gym membership prices due to increased demand and premium amenities.

  • Comparing contracts, hidden fees and available discounts is crucial to understanding the real cost of a gym.

  • Annual memberships can offer savings over monthly options, but only if you’re confident you’ll use them for a full year.

  • Look for affordable gym options in the UK by exploring student, NHS and corporate deals, or off-peak memberships.

Gym Membership Costs in the UK: Main Types and Price Ranges

Prices depend on the type of gym and on the branch. The figures below were read on each chain’s own website in November 2026. Every chain says prices vary by location, so treat them as starting points and check the club nearest to you.

Budget Gyms

The Gym Group lists off-peak membership from £15.99 a month, Standard from £19.99 and Ultimate from £24.99, with a joining fee from £10. PureGym lists Off-peak from £17.99, Core from £22.99 and Plus from £28.99 a month, plus a joining fee. Both offer monthly plans with no long contract, and extras such as personal training cost more.

Mid-Range Fitness Clubs

Health clubs with pools and spa areas cost more. At Bannatyne’s Mansfield club, Full Access is listed from £59.99 a month and Off Peak (weekdays, 9am to 4.30pm) from £49.99, with pools, saunas, steam rooms and classes included. Nuffield Health’s Leatherhead gym lists £87 a month for anytime access and £74 for off-peak on a 12-month commitment, plus a £29 joining fee.

Premium and Boutique Clubs

Premium clubs such as David Lloyd and Third Space don’t publish one national price list, so ask your local club for a written quote. Expect to pay well above health-club prices, especially in London, for extras such as spa access, tennis courts and family facilities. Boutique studios for yoga, Pilates or HIIT usually charge per class or per bundle, which adds up if you go several times a week. If relaxation is part of the plan, see our guide to meditation apps in the UK.

The most significant price drivers are typically location, range of facilities, contract flexibility and the inclusion of classes.

Monthly vs Annual Gym Memberships: Which Offers Better Value?

The choice between a monthly and an annual membership changes what you pay. Monthly contracts give you flexibility, while 12-month commitments are usually cheaper per month if you keep going.

Monthly Memberships

Most affordable gym options in the UK offer rolling monthly memberships, usually without a minimum contract. These are ideal if you want the freedom to cancel or pause your membership with little notice. Typical monthly fees include all equipment access but may charge extra for classes or additional services. It’s worth noting that monthly fees are usually higher per month than annual equivalents.

Annual Memberships

Annual contracts tie you in for 12 months and may be paid upfront or by direct debit. Many clubs price them below their rolling monthly rate, which suits regular gym-goers. If you leave early, Citizens Advice says you will normally have to pay the full cost of the contract. How to Compare Gym Memberships in the United Kingdom

The headline price is only part of the picture. It’s about finding the right fit for your lifestyle, location, and wellness priorities. Here’s what to look for in a gym membership to ensure you get the best mix of value and experience.

Facilities, Classes, and Extras

Ask yourself what matters most: Do you need a pool or sauna? Are group exercise classes included, or do they cost extra? Is 24-hour access important for your schedule? Not all gyms include the same amenities in their base price. For example, budget gyms may charge for classes or locker hire, while premium clubs might bundle everything into one rate.

If you want one-to-one coaching on top of your membership, budget for it separately. Our guide to the cost of personal trainers in London sets out what to expect.

Location and Accessibility

Your local gym pricing comparison should take into account commute time and opening hours. A gym near your home or workplace in the United Kingdom increases the likelihood you’ll stick to your fitness goals. Some multi-site gym chains offer access to multiple branches, which is helpful if you travel frequently.

Reviews and Word of Mouth

Online reviews and word-of-mouth recommendations are invaluable. Check what other members say about cleanliness, staff, maintenance, and class quality. This can highlight hidden issues and help you spot a gym that is genuinely good value.

Hidden Gym Fees and How to Avoid Them

Hidden fees are a common frustration. Many gyms advertise low monthly rates but charge extra for essentials or include lengthy notice periods in their contracts. Here’s what to watch for:

Common Hidden Fees

Typical charges include joining fees (from £10 at The Gym Group and £29 at Nuffield Health Leatherhead when we checked), inductions, locker rentals, towel hire, or extra costs for certain group classes. Some clubs also charge for access to premium facilities or have peak/off-peak pricing tiers. Always request a written breakdown of all costs before signing.

Cancellation fees are another pitfall. Even with rolling contracts you will usually need to give notice, and Citizens Advice says that if you leave a fixed-term contract early you will normally have to pay the full cost. Read every term and ask questions before committing.

Getting Gym Membership Discounts UK

Some gyms offer discounts for students, NHS staff, over-60s or corporate partners, and many run seasonal promotions or refer-a-friend deals, so it’s worth asking. Off-peak memberships restrict you to quieter times but cost less: The Gym Group’s off-peak plan starts at £15.99 a month against £19.99 for Standard.

Gym Prices in London

London has the widest choice of gyms in the UK, from budget chains to luxury clubs, and prices are higher than the national starting rates. Here’s what we found:

Budget and Mid-Range Gyms

PureGym’s London Marylebone gym lists Off-Peak at £32.99, Core at £41.99 and Plus at £52.99 a month, with a £19.99 joining fee, nearly double the chain’s national starting prices. Health clubs with pools and classes cost more again, and each location sets its own rates.

Premium and Boutique Clubs

David Lloyd, Third Space and Virgin Active sit at the top of the London market and price by club and membership tier, so ask for a written quote. Boutique studios such as Barry’s or Psycle sell classes one at a time or in bundles. If you mostly go for classes, compare the cost per class with a full gym membership.

London’s competitive market means clubs frequently offer short-term deals, so it’s worth shopping around or negotiating, especially if you’re willing to commit for a year.

What to Look for in a Gym Contract and Cancellation Policies UK

Gym contracts are covered by consumer law, which Trading Standards and the Competition and Markets Authority enforce. Even so, terms and cancellation options vary widely, so read the small print and ask questions before joining.

Understanding Contracts

Check minimum term commitments, notice periods (Citizens Advice gives 30 days as an example), and any early exit charges. Some gyms allow you to freeze your membership for a fee—useful if you travel or are injured. Others charge a re-joining fee if you leave and return later. A good contract should be transparent, with no hidden clauses.

Consumer Rights and Protection

If a term looks unfair, such as automatic renewal without your permission or a minimum term of more than a year, you may be able to challenge it under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Citizens Advice also says a gym should usually let you cancel if a serious injury or illness stops you exercising. Keep a copy of your agreement and any correspondence, and see Citizens Advice’s guide to cancelling a gym membership for next steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a typical gym membership cost in the UK in 2026?

Budget chains start from £15.99 (The Gym Group, off-peak) and £17.99 (PureGym, off-peak) a month, while the health clubs we checked cost roughly £50–£90 a month. London prices are higher, and every chain says prices vary by location.

What’s the best way to compare gym membership costs?

List your priorities (facilities, classes, location), gather prices from local gyms, ask about discounts and hidden fees, and compare contract terms. Reviews and recommendations can help you spot the best value in your area.

How can I avoid hidden fees in gym contracts?

Ask for a detailed breakdown of all charges, including joining fees, class costs, and cancellation terms. Read the contract thoroughly and don’t be afraid to ask questions or request clarification before signing.

Are there gym discounts for certain groups?

Some gyms offer discounts for students, NHS staff, corporate teams, families or over-60s. Ask your local club and check for seasonal offers or referral deals.

Should I choose a monthly or annual gym membership?

Annual memberships are usually cheaper per month, but only make sense if you’re certain you will use the gym regularly. Monthly options are more flexible and may be better for those unsure of their long-term plans.

A careful cost comparison of gym memberships in the UK means checking the monthly fee, joining fee and contract terms at the clubs near you, not just the headline price.
