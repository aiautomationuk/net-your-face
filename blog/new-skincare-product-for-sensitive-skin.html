New Skincare Product for Sensitive Skin

27 November 2026

The safest way to add a new skincare product for sensitive skin is slowly: read the ingredient list, patch test a small amount for a few days, then bring it into your routine on its own before adding anything else. Going one product at a time means that if your skin reacts, you know exactly what caused it.

Quick answer: Check the ingredients for anything that has bothered you before, then apply a little of the product to a small patch of skin, such as behind the ear or on the inner forearm, once or twice a day for several days. If there is no redness, itching or swelling, start using it on your face a few times a week, and wait a couple of weeks before you add another new product.

Key Takeaways

  • Change one product at a time, so you can tell what suits your skin and what does not.

  • Patch test on a small area for several days before you use a product on your whole face.

  • In the UK, cosmetics must list their ingredients, so you can check for known triggers.

  • Fragrance and some essential oils are common culprits for sensitive skin.

  • Stop using anything that causes a rash, swelling or blistering, and speak to a pharmacist or GP if it does not settle.

Why Sensitive Skin Needs a Slower Approach

Sensitive skin reacts more easily to products, weather and friction. It may sting, flush or feel tight after cleansing, or come up in small red patches after a new cream. Mature skin can become drier and more reactive over time too, so a routine that worked for years may start to feel uncomfortable.

The temptation with a new routine is to buy several products and start them all on the same morning. If your skin then reacts, you have no idea which one is responsible. Slowing down feels frustrating, but it saves you money and discomfort in the long run.

Read the Ingredient List First

Cosmetics sold in the UK must show their ingredients on the pack or with the product, using standard international names. That list is your first check. If you have reacted to something before, look for it by name. Ingredients are listed broadly in order of quantity, so anything near the top makes up more of the product.

Fragrance is one of the most common causes of irritation. It can appear as "parfum" or "fragrance", and some essential oils carry naturally occurring fragrance allergens, such as limonene and linalool, which are often named at the end of the list. That does not mean every scented product will bother you, but it is worth knowing what to look for. You might also like our guide on top-rated wellness retreats in the UK.

How to Patch Test

  • Choose a small area of skin, such as behind your ear, the side of your neck or your inner forearm.

  • Apply a small amount of the product as you would normally use it.

  • Repeat once or twice a day for several days, unless the pack gives different directions.

  • Watch for redness, itching, burning, swelling or small bumps.

  • No reaction: start using it on your face, at first every other day.

  • Mild tingling that fades quickly: carry on carefully and stop if it gets worse.

  • Redness, itching or swelling: stop using it and wash the area with water.

A patch test cannot rule out every reaction, because the skin on your face is thinner than on your arm. It does catch many problems before they spread across your face, though. A smaller trial size is useful here, as you are not left with a full jar you cannot use.

Bring a New Skincare Product for Sensitive Skin Into Your Routine

Once the patch test is clear, use the new product a few times a week for the first week or two, then build up. Keep the rest of your routine the same. A simple base of a gentle cleanser, a moisturiser and sunscreen in the day gives you a steady background, so you can see what the new product changes.

Signs of a Reaction

Stop using a product if your skin becomes red, swollen, itchy or blistered, or if it burns rather than tingles. Rinse with cool water and go back to your simple base routine. Most mild reactions settle within a few days once the product is stopped. If a reaction is severe, spreads or does not settle, speak to a pharmacist or your GP. Make-up and Sensitive Skin

The same rules apply to make-up. Add one new item at a time and remove it gently at the end of the day. Our natural make-up look guide shows how to keep a light, simple base that is easier on reactive skin.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a skincare patch test last?

Several days of repeated use gives a better picture than a single application, because some reactions take time to appear. Follow any directions on the pack.

Is natural or organic skincare better for sensitive skin?

Not automatically. Plant extracts and essential oils can irritate sensitive skin too. Read the ingredient list and patch test any product, whatever it is made from. If you would like some help with this, get in touch with our team.

Can I use more than one new product at once?

It is better not to. Adding one product at a time, with a gap of a couple of weeks, shows you clearly what works for your skin.
